# Trainchecker: handover, November 17th 2026

John's own words, copied unchanged from the Claude Code thread of November
17th 2026 (the session that also added the idea to the project tracker).
There was no separate handover file; these messages are it.

## 1. John's question in a Claude chat, and the chat's reply

John: "Is there any gap in the market for a baish.net website that uses
data about the trains. I wondered if it's worth a general "How's it looking
on this line (from A to B)" really simple thing?" Draft name Trainchecker.

The chat's reply, as John pasted it:

> I'll check what already exists in this space before giving you a view.
> There's a gap, but only a narrow one. "Live trains from A to B right now" is very well covered already, so a site doing just that wouldn't add much. The opening I can see is a plain-English verdict that combines right now with how this line has been lately. Nobody seems to do that in one simple glance.
> What's already out there
>
> * Live status. The National Rail Enquiries app already lets you save a journey and get delay alerts. Realtime Trains, Trainline and lots of smaller apps cover the rest. Some are deliberately minimal: one Android app does only one thing - give live train times (no journey planner etc.), and another is Trackd – Live GB Rail Status, which shows the whole network. You'd be up against free, polished, well-known tools.
> * Recent punctuality. Recent Train Times is still running. When I checked, its data went up to yesterday evening. It contains the actual times of all passenger trains that have run in England, Wales and Scotland over the past three months. For any A-to-B pair it gives percentages of arrivals on time or within set delays, a performance score, and cancellation counts. It does the job well, but the screen is dense, with lots of dropdowns, and it only covers direct trains.
>
> Where a baish.net site could fit
> Something in the style of your Lightreader sentences, for example: "Reading → Paddington, 9am: the next three trains are on time. This week, 8 of 10 morning trains arrived within 3 minutes. Thursday was the bad day." That's one postcode-free screen with no app, no ads and no settings. The existing tools each cover half of this. None of them gives you both halves in one sentence a normal person can read.
> The data is free and the licence looks workable. National Rail says Darwin data feeds are openly available to everyone. Registering for access couldn't be easier, simply register via the Rail Data Marketplace. That covers the live departure board API and the historical feed. The history covers more than you'd need: Darwin powers a Historical Service Performance platform that shows the real-time historic performance of trains over periods of up to one year. The open-rail-data community has reported occasional outages on the Rail Data Marketplace this year, so it isn't bulletproof. I haven't tested how fast or reliable the historical API is, and I'd want to check that before committing.
> My view: this is worth doing if you'd use it yourself on a line you know, as a neat small tool in your house style. It's unlikely to stand out the way Riskreckoner or Riverwatcher could, because this is the most crowded space of all your ideas. If you go ahead, I'd make the "how's it been this week" verdict the main feature and keep the live part secondary, because that's the part nobody presents simply.
> Sources:
>
> * [Recent Train Times](https://recenttraintimes.co.uk/Home/Index?SmpFm=1)
> * [National Rail – Darwin data feeds](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/100296.aspx)
> * [data.gov.uk – Real-time and historic train information](https://www.data.gov.uk/collections/transport/real-time-and-historic-train-information)
> * [RailForums – journey notification apps](https://www.railforums.co.uk/threads/what-is-the-best-app-for-journey-notifications.272937)
> * [Trackd – App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6748082848)

## 2. John's brief, to carry it into development

> Could we please carry Trainchecker forward now into development? Tagline: "How's this train line doing". Intended audience: People who are travelling later today, or in the next few days, who want a general overview in plain English of what's going on with the line. Also, people who want to be broken in gently when it comes to bad news - so maybe we start with a friendly verdict - "Good news..." or "It's a mixed bag..." or "Be prepared". I'd like it to tell me in words about departures for the next five hours with a summary of how they're looking and details if any are delayed or cancelled. It could also tell me which platforms those trains are going from at the moment, generally speaking. Then the reliability of those services over the last week, and a look ahead to any engineering work which is causing known issues in the next few days. For any service which is badly disrupted, an alternative route if one is available, and roughly how long it will take. If you enter two stations which aren't direct, it would work out the change.
>
> I've created the Trainchecker repo. I haven't done anything else yet.
