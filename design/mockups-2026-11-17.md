## Questions for John (M1 to M20)

1. **M1. The verdicts.** Are the four openings right as drafted above ("Good news: trains
   from … are running well this evening.", "It's a mixed bag: most trains … are running
   this evening, but two of the 18 in the next four hours are cancelled.", "Be prepared:
   trains … are disrupted this evening. Of the next 30, 10 are cancelled …, mostly
   because of …", and "badly disrupted" when the agreed "badly disrupted" rule is met)?
   "This morning / afternoon / evening" follows the clock (before midday, before 5pm, after).
2. **M2. When "However, it's worth knowing…" appears** (Claude's rule): when the last two
   hours had two or more cancellations, or fewer than three in four trains within 3
   minutes, and the verdict isn't already "Be prepared". After "It's a mixed bag" it reads
   "It's also worth knowing…". Agree, or another line?
3. **M3. A train shown only as "Delayed"** (no time yet) counts as late for the verdict, as
   in stage 1's trial. Agree?
4. **M4. Train times:** "6.26pm" (house style), or "18:26" as on station boards and
   tickets? Claude's suggestion: "6.26pm", for consistency with the other sites; but
   "18:26" matches what people see on the platform.
5. **M5. National Rail's reasons:** shown without their opening words ("Train crew being
   delayed by service disruption."), or in full ("This service has been delayed by train
   crew being delayed by service disruption.")?
6. **M6. Long lists on a phone:** Manchester Airport has 12 trains not on time, about
   1,500px of scrolling on a phone. Show them all (as now), or the first five and "Show
   the other seven"?
7. **M7. The next four hours, platforms and the last two hours:** the sentences above.
   Note that sentences may start with a number ("46 are expected on time"); fine, or
   reword ("Of these, 46 are…")?
8. **M8. "On time" in the summary is within 3 minutes; the list shows trains more than 5
   minutes late** (the agreed verdict rule). Trains 4 or 5 minutes late are counted as "a
   few minutes late" in the sentence but not listed. Agree?
9. **M9. Which engineering work counts** (Claude's first rule, to test properly in stage 4,
   as National Rail describes routes in words only): the item names both stations, or one
   "between … and …" in its routes names both, or it's "to / from / via" one of them; a
   single amended train counts only in its own direction. It gives the lists above. Most
   of Paddington's are very early or very late trains. Show them all (as now), or only those
   National Rail marks as affecting daytime trains (not possible to tell reliably from its
   words)?
10. **M10. National Rail's advice is folded**, even in "Be prepared": opened, Manchester's
    is about 2,500px long on a phone. Agree, or open it when the journey is badly
    disrupted?
11. **M11. The Disruptions API may not be needed.** Tonight's unplanned Manchester
    disruption, with National Rail's full advice (Metrolink, buses, tickets accepted), was in
    the Knowledgebase Incidents feed too. Suggest: test both in stage 4 and keep only one if

12. **M12. The last week:** the hours list (default: the current hour), the loading line,
    the result sentence and the Elizabeth line note, as above.
13. **M13. The Share button** (house style §17): Claude suggests words and a link, no picture

14. **M14. The address after "#":** station codes, `trainchecker.baish.net/#RDG-PAD`. Agree?
15. **M15. "Check again"** to fetch the trains afresh, with the time they were fetched. Agree?

17. **M17. About these figures, the credit and the privacy line**, as above. This is the first

18. **M18. Colours:** a coloured edge down the verdict card (olive for good news, terracotta

## John's first answers (November 17th 2026, evening)

1. **"Good news, however…" (M1, M2):** keep the classification, change the words. What it
   means: trains are leaving on time but haven't necessarily been getting there on time.
   John's formulation: "Good news: trains from Reading to London Paddington are leaving on
   time this evening." Then, on a new line: "Half of the trains on this route in the last
   couple of hours got to London Paddington within three minutes of their timetabled
   arrival. The others were an average of x minutes late." ("three", not "3".) With
   tonight's figures, x is 8 (11 trains, 4 to 23 minutes late).
2. **Rounding wherever possible**, so sentences read naturally: "a quarter", "a third",
   "half", "two thirds", "three quarters", with "about"; also "one in ten" and "one in
   twenty".
3. **The next four hours:** no times in the first sentence (not "between 6.24pm and
   10.24pm"); summarise the trains that aren't on time, and the reasons.
4. **Platforms:** start "At the moment, most trains are going from…"; drop "Platforms for
   later trains aren't announced yet."
5. **The last two hours:** not shown as its own section when the top section has given it.
6. **The strip's label:** "TRAINS TO CHECK", not "YOUR JOURNEY" (it may be someone else's
   journey).
7. **The chooser's button:** "Show trains", not "Show the trains".
